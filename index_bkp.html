How to read the stock overview

The stock overview brings together price trends, momentum, volatility, market-cycle position and performance relative to the market. Its purpose is to help you assess three things:

  • Trend — is the stock moving up, moving down, or losing direction?
  • Phase — is the move developing, continuing, pulling back, or showing signs of exhaustion?
  • Relative strength — is the stock performing better or worse than the broader market?

How to start

  1. Read the market state. It gives you a quick summary of the current technical picture.
  2. Check the weekly and daily trends. The weekly trend provides the longer-term context; the daily trend shows shorter-term developments.
  3. Review energy and cycle position. These help distinguish a developing move from a mature move, a pause, or a possible reversal.
  4. Compare performance with the market. A stock that outperforms the index may deserve more attention than one that is lagging.
  5. Look for conflicting evidence. A strong score does not remove risk, and a possible reversal is not a confirmed new trend.

The report is a decision-support tool, not a prediction or a buy/sell instruction. Consider your investment horizon, valuation, company fundamentals and risk exposure before making a decision.

Optional: you can paste a report into an AI assistant and ask a focused question such as “what supports this assessment, what contradicts it, and what evidence would confirm a reversal?” AI interpretation can be wrong and is not required to use the report.

Reading the vocabulary

Trend labels

UPTREND price structure points up. DOWNTREND points down. SIDEWAYS and NEUTRAL mean no clear direction. EARLY_PULLBACK is an uptrend with a shallow dip. EARLY_RECOVERY is a downtrend with a shallow bounce.

Patterns

Confirmed Trend the trend is intact. Leader Pullback a dip inside a long uptrend. Early Bull Reversal the first sign a decline may turn. Confirmed Downtrend the decline is intact. Trend Losing Momentum the push is fading. Failed Recovery a bounce attempt already broke. Countertrend Rally / Recovery Attempt a bounce against the main trend.

Market states summarise the broader technical picture; pattern labels describe a particular trend or price behaviour within that picture.

Energy

COMPRESSION energy coiling. RELEASED energy discharging. EXHAUSTION the move may be spent. NONE no energy signal. VOLATILE unusually jumpy. Energy has no direction: it says how much is stored, not which way it will go.

Band zones

Where the close sits inside the Bollinger bands: L below the lower band, LM the lower half, M on the middle line, MU the upper half, U above the upper band. A close above the upper band does not automatically mean a buy, and a close below the lower band does not automatically mean a sell.

Trend strength

A 0–100 gauge (the ADX) of how strongly directional the market is: below 20 weak, 20–30 building, 30–40 strong, 40+ very strong. Trend strength is not the same as bullishness — a strong downtrend can also show high trend strength.

Reading names

A plus joins timeframe + indicator + length. A leading w means weekly, m monthly, no prefix daily. So wMA200 is the weekly 200-period average, mMA the monthly middle band line, MA9 the daily 9-period average.

Worked examples

Example 1 — a neutral daily picture inside a bearish longer-term trend.

Weekly : DOWNTREND  Daily : NEUTRAL  Energy : NONE
State : PAUSE  Score : 30  Conf : 55%
Summary : The longer-term trend remains bearish while the daily picture is
          currently neutral. With no clear energy signal, the setup is PAUSE.

The weekly (longer-term) trend is bearish, but the daily trend is neutral and there is no energy signal, so the report classifies the setup as PAUSE. This is a continuing bearish longer-term trend with a neutral daily picture — it is not the same as a bearish trend confirmed on every timeframe.

Example 2 — a possible regime change.

Weekly : DOWNTREND  Daily : UPTREND  Energy : EXHAUSTION
State : TRANSITION  Score : 62  Conf : 61%
Summary : Downtrend exhausting — regime changing.

A bearish weekly structure meets a fast daily recovery with spent energy. The engine reports TRANSITION: a possible change in regime. This is not evidence that a reversal has already occurred — wait for confirmation of which direction prevails.

Example 3 — conflicting timeframes (a pullback, not a reversal).

Weekly : UPTREND   Daily : DOWNTREND  Energy : NONE
State : PULLBACK  Summary : Longer-term uptrend intact while the daily trend corrects.

Here the weekly trend is still bullish while the daily trend is bearish. That combination is a PULLBACK, not a reversal: a short-term setback can coexist with a longer-term uptrend. A change of state is not automatically a trend reversal — watch whether the longer-term structure holds.

Score and confidence

Score (0–100) summarises the strength and direction of the technical assessment. It combines the weekly trend, daily trend, energy conditions and relative performance.

Confidence (0–100%) describes how consistently the available signals support the assessment. It is not the probability that the stock will rise, the probability of a profitable trade, or a guarantee that the assessment is correct.

How to interpret them together

  • Strong score, high confidence: the technical assessment is strong and the evidence is relatively consistent.
  • Strong score, low confidence: the assessment looks favourable, but the supporting evidence is less reliable or less consistent. Check for conflicting signals.
  • Moderate score, high confidence: the assessment may be clear, but its direction or strength is not especially favourable.
  • Low score, low confidence: the picture is weak or uncertain. Avoid drawing a strong conclusion from the score alone.

Always read the score alongside the market state, the weekly and daily trends, and the supporting evidence. A high score is not automatically a buy signal.

Cycle position

The cycle block shows where price sits inside its current swing. A swing high or swing low is a significant turning point, found by ignoring day-to-day noise; the last swing point is the most recent such turning point.

Stages: ADVANCE pushing up, PULLBACK dipping inside an advance, DECLINE pushing down, RECOVERY bouncing inside a decline. The block also shows how far the close has travelled from the last swing point and whether the weekly, daily and monthly swings agree.

Performance versus the index

Returns over 1 week, 1 month and 1 year sit next to the index return for the same window, plus the gap between the two. A positive gap means the stock outperformed the index over that window; a negative gap means it lagged. A stock that consistently outperforms may deserve more attention than one that is lagging, and the gap also nudges the score.

Volume and relative strength

These figures describe price position, relative strength and trading activity. On their own they do not establish institutional buying or selling; treat them as indirect market-activity indicators.

Pwh
Percent distance of the close below the last weekly high. 0 means at the high; −7.3 means 7.3% under it.
Pwh_rel
The stock’s Pwh minus the index’s, in percentage points. Positive means nearer its high than the index; negative means lagging it.
VolRatio
This week’s volume against its recent average. Above 1 means busier than usual.
DAX Ratio
How the stock’s recent path tracks the index path. Near 1 moves together; well above or below means decoupling.
1d Ret
Last daily return in percent.

Trend report columns

The weekly pivot trend section lists, per stock: the zone pair (wL -> wMU), the age from the pivot day to today (days, or weeks as 6W), the performance since the pivot in percent, the highest price seen since the pivot (lowest price for down moves), its distance to the current close in percent, and the gap to the index return over the same window in percent.

Glossary

Price and averages

Moving averages
MA is the 20-period average (same as the middle band line), MA9 the fast 9-period line, MA50 and MA200 the slow lines. A leading w/m switches to weekly/monthly.
PLH and PLL
Percentage change from the last swing high (PLH) or last swing low (PLL) to the latest close. PLH_1 and PLL_1 do the same for the turning points before those.
Pwh and Pmh
Distance of the close from the last weekly high (Pwh) or last monthly high (Pmh), in percent. See Pwh_rel above for the index-relative form.

Bands and volatility

Bollinger bands
The middle line is a 20-period moving average. The upper and lower bands sit two standard deviations above and below it. The half-width is (upper − lower) ÷ 2.
Volatility ratio
The latest 14-bar price standard deviation as a percentage of its own average: below 80 low, 80 to 120 normal, above 120 high.

Swing structure

Zigzag, swing, leg
The zigzag keeps only significant turning points and drops the noise between them. Each turning point is a swing high (H) or swing low (L). A leg is the move between two consecutive turning points.

Naming and log format

One-line log
A line such as =<[]D[]D[]noX has four parts split by []: band shape, fast-average direction, slow-average direction, and the expected cross (noX means none). Direction codes: U up, V turned up, D down, A turned down. By default the fast and slow averages are wMA9 and wMA on weekly, MA9 and MA on daily.
Swing text
Strings such as H:2026-07-13 MU 2.2% 4.0/8.9|-|L::2026-07-15 MU 1.4% 2.5/8.1. Each point gives its type and date, the band zone, the close’s percentage distance from the middle line, and its absolute distance over the band half-width. A double colon marks the newest, still-open point.
