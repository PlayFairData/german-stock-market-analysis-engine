Market Context – Sector Rotation

Sector rotation provides relative-strength context. It does not generate independent buy or sell signals.

  1. Identify improving areas. Sectors with improving relative strength, breadth or momentum deserve closer attention. They are candidates for further stock-level analysis, not automatic buying opportunities.
  2. Identify deteriorating areas. Weakening relative strength, breadth and momentum can indicate increasing sector risk. Review existing holdings for weakening individual price structure and fundamentals.
  3. Compare sector and stock evidence. A strong stock in a weak sector may still be attractive, but requires stronger individual evidence. A weak stock in a leading sector may indicate a stock-specific problem.
  4. Use rotation to prioritise decisions. Combine sector context with the stock overview, EPS trend and smart-money activity. The individual stock setup determines the action; sector rotation provides supporting or conflicting context.
