How to read the stock state

The overview looks at a stock from several independent angles and combines them into one market state, with a score, a confidence level and the reasons behind it. This page explains the terms and how the pieces fit together.

Reading the names

Indicator names follow one pattern: a timeframe prefix, an indicator, and often a period.

wMA200the 200-week moving average

No prefix means daily, w means weekly and m means monthly. Bollinger bands are a middle line, which is a moving average, with an upper and a lower band set above and below it according to recent price variability. Wide bands mean a volatile market and narrow bands a quiet one. A plain MA without a number is that middle line, so wMA is the weekly middle line and wMA9 is the faster 9-week average. UB and LB are the upper and lower bands.

Trend: weekly and daily

The report shows the same four fields for the weekly and the daily trend, plus how the two relate.

Trend
The overall direction, summarised as bull, bear or neutral.
ADX
How strong the trend is, regardless of its direction: weak, building, strong or very strong.
MA
How the fast and slow moving averages behave. Up means they rise together, Down means they fall together, and Mixed means they disagree or are turning.
BB
The shape of the Bollinger bands: expanding when they widen and volatility rises, squeezing when they narrow, drifting when both bands move the same way.
Trend relation
How the daily trend sits against the weekly one: confirmation when they agree, pullback when weekly is up and daily is down, pause when weekly is up and daily is sideways, counter-trend bounce when weekly is down and daily is up, stabilizing when weekly is down and daily is sideways, and undecided when the weekly trend is neutral.

Energy

Energy describes how much movement is stored up in a stock or has just been released. It has no direction of its own: the trend readings say which way, energy says how much. The report shows a family and the specific state inside it.

FamilyStateMeaning
compressionWeak compressionPrice is quiet or moving sideways, and a little energy may be gathering.
CompressionThe same picture with clearer evidence that energy is gathering.
Energy buildingTrend strength is starting to pick up from a low level while volatility stays tight.
releasedEnergy releasedAn active trend with widening moves: stored energy has turned into a move.
exhaustionTrend exhaustionA strong trend that is losing steam, with fading strength or shrinking volatility.
volatileHigh volatilityLarge, erratic swings without clear directional control. Confidence is reduced.
noneNone, failed compressionNothing notable, or a squeeze that broke without follow-through. Both count as no energy signal.

Cycle

The trend readings ask what the trend is. The cycle reading asks where price sits inside its current swing. A swing high or low is a turning point, and the zigzag keeps only the significant ones. A leg is the move between two consecutive turning points. The cycle reading names the last leg and checks where price is now relative to its end.

Last legPrice nowStage
Low to highstill above that highADVANCE
Low to highback below that highPULLBACK
High to lowstill below that lowDECLINE
High to lowback above that lowRECOVERY

The stage states a fact, not a view. Whether a recovery is a real turn or only a bounce in a downtrend is judged in the overview.

Alignment
The daily, weekly and monthly views each have their own last leg. Alignment counts how many of the three run in the same direction as the daily leg. The daily view always counts itself, so the value runs from 1 to 3. If the daily leg runs high to low while the weekly and monthly legs both run low to high, alignment is 1.
Swing age
The number of days since the last turning point of the daily leg.
Legs
The direction of the last leg in each view. H_TO_L means price ran from a high down to a low, and L_TO_H from a low up to a high.

Performance

Shown only when a time period is chosen. It compares the stock's return over that period with the DAX and with its sector, and tells you whether the stock is leading or lagging.

From readings to a state

The state describes what kind of setup this is. The score then ranks how strong the setup is compared with others in the same state, and never overrides the state.

StateWhat it usually means
BREAKOUTWeekly and daily trends both up, with energy stored up or just released.
CONTINUATIONWeekly and daily trends both up, without a fresh build-up of energy.
PULLBACKThe weekly trend is intact while the daily trend corrects. Also used when the weekly trend is down and the daily trend has not yet confirmed a bounce.
PAUSEThe weekly trend is intact and the daily trend moves sideways, often while energy builds.
COUNTERTRENDThe weekly trend is down but the daily trend is bouncing, or a downtrend is losing steam.
REVERSALWeekly and daily trends are both down. The label marks a bearish structure rather than a confirmed turn.
TRANSITIONThe evidence is mixed and the combination fits none of the states above.
Score
Ranks a setup against others in the same state. The weekly trend counts most, then the daily trend, then energy. Outperforming or underperforming the index nudges it slightly.
Confidence
How reliable the underlying readings are. One weak reading pulls it down even when the others are strong. It rises a little when all timeframes agree on the current swing, falls when only the daily one does, and drops sharply when volatility is extreme.
What cycle does not do
It never changes the state or the score. It only adjusts confidence and adds a line to the reasons.

How to start

Read the report in a fixed order. Each step narrows the picture; skipping one is how a good-looking score turns into a poor decision.

  1. Read the market state. It summarises the current technical picture in one word.
  2. Compare the weekly and daily trends. The weekly trend gives the longer-term context; the daily trend shows what price is doing right now.
  3. Review energy and cycle position. These separate a developing move from a mature one, a pause, or a possible reversal.
  4. Compare performance with the market. A stock beating its sector and the index deserves more attention than one that is lagging.
  5. Look for conflicting evidence. A high score does not remove risk, and a possible reversal is not a confirmed new trend.

The report is a decision-support tool, not a prediction or a buy/sell instruction. Weigh your investment horizon, valuation, company fundamentals and risk exposure before acting.

Optional: paste a report into an AI assistant and ask a focused question, such as “what supports this assessment, what contradicts it, and what evidence would confirm a reversal?” AI interpretation can be wrong and is not required to use the report. See an example chat with ChatGPT.

Layers of the report

The report reads the same stock through a few independent views. Two describe the trend, one describes stored energy, and two supply supporting context. Only three of the five actually decide the state.

Weekly trend (long-term)Where is the slower, longer-term trend?decides the state
Daily trend (short-term)What is price doing right now?decides the state
EnergyIs the move compressed, released, or exhausted?decides the state
Cycle position (supporting)Where is price inside its current swing?adjusts confidence only
Performance vs. index (supporting)Is the stock beating or lagging the market?nudges the score only
